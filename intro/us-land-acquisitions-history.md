## The Northwest Territory

The Northwest Territory consisted principally of the land:

* west of Pennsylvania;
* north and west of the Ohio River;
* east of the Mississippi River;
* and south of British Canada.

It ultimately included all or most of present-day **Ohio, Indiana, Illinois, Michigan, Wisconsin, and northeastern Minnesota**. The Library of Congress describes it as territory established by Congress in 1787 north and west of the Ohio River. ([The Library of Congress][2])

The McConnell map above shows that territory in relation to the state cessions. It is therefore probably more useful for your purpose than a map that shows only the Northwest Territory by itself.

### Date of the Northwest Ordinance

The **Northwest Ordinance was enacted—more precisely, adopted—on July 13, 1787**, by the Confederation Congress operating under the Articles of Confederation. Its formal title was:

> *An Ordinance for the Government of the Territory of the United States North-West of the River Ohio.*

It established the territorial government and the process by which the territory could be divided into states and admitted to the Union. ([National Archives][3])

The sequence was therefore:

1. The Treaty of Paris in **1783** gave the United States an international claim to the land east of the Mississippi.
2. Individual states surrendered their overlapping western claims, principally during the **1780s**.
3. Congress passed the Land Ordinance of **1785**, creating the basic federal survey-and-sale system.
4. Congress adopted the Northwest Ordinance on **July 13, 1787**, organizing the territory politically.

The state cessions did not transfer the land from foreign ownership to the United States; Britain had already recognized the United States’ claim in 1783. The cessions settled the competing claims **within the United States** between the individual states and the national government.
